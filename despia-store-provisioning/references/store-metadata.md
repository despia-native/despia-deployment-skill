# Store metadata and ASO

Most Despia users are founders, not marketers. They will either leave these fields near-empty or paste their website's homepage copy in, and both cost them installs on a store where the text *is* the discovery mechanism. Write the copy for them, then have them approve it.

## Finding out what the app is

Four questions. Don't send a brief - ask them like a person:

1. **What does your app do, in a sentence you'd say to a friend?** Take their words verbatim; founders describe their own product better than they think, and that phrasing is usually the subtitle.
2. **Who is it for?** Specific beats broad. "Freelance photographers" gives you keywords, "everyone" gives you nothing.
3. **What would someone type into the App Store to find an app like yours?** This is keyword research disguised as a question they can actually answer.
4. **What's the one thing it does better than the alternatives?** Goes in the opening line of the description.

If they have a website, read it first and come with a draft rather than an empty form. Reacting to copy is far easier than producing it, and a founder who says "no, it's more like X" has just given you better copy than any question would have.

## Apple: what's indexed and what isn't

| Field | Limit | Indexed? |
|---|---|---|
| App name | 30 | Yes - highest weight |
| Subtitle | 30 | Yes - high weight |
| Keyword field | 100 | Yes, and invisible to users |
| Description | 4000 | **No** - not indexed at all |
| Promotional text | 170 | No - but editable without a new build |

The description does no ranking work on the App Store. It converts a visitor who is already looking at the page. Write it for a human, not a crawler.

Keyword field rules that people get wrong:

- Comma-separated, **no spaces after commas** - a space costs a character and you only get 100
- Never repeat a word that's already in the name or subtitle; Apple combines them
- Singular or plural, not both - Apple matches across them
- Skip "app", "free", your own category name, and anything Apple already knows from your category
- No competitor brand names - it's against the rules and gets rejected
- Multi-word phrases form automatically from single words, so `photo,editor,filter` covers "photo editor" and "photo filter"

## Google: what's indexed and what isn't

| Field | Limit | Indexed? |
|---|---|---|
| App title | 30 | Yes - highest weight |
| Short description | 80 | Yes - high weight |
| Full description | 4000 | **Yes** |

Opposite of Apple. There's no keyword field, so keywords live in the description text. Use the primary keyword a handful of times across 4000 characters, naturally, in sentences that read normally. Play penalises stuffing and users bounce off it anyway.

Because the two stores index differently, **don't reuse one set of copy for both.** Same positioning, different construction.

## Writing the copy

**Name.** Brand first. If there's room, one descriptive phrase after a separator - "Lumen: Photo Editor". Don't burn the 30 characters on a tagline.

**Subtitle / short description.** The single clearest statement of what it does and for whom. No exclamation marks, no "the best". This is the line that decides whether someone taps.

**Description.** First two lines are all most people see before "more":

- Open with the outcome, not the technology
- Then a short scannable feature list
- Then trust: who it's for, what it costs, links
- Plain language. If the founder is an engineer, actively pull them back from feature-listing

**Promotional text (Apple)** is the one field editable without a build. Useful for "now with X" - tell them it exists, most people never notice it.

## Keyword research without tools

You probably don't have an ASO tool. You can still do better than guessing:

- Search the App Store and Play for the terms from question 3 and read what the top results use in their names and subtitles
- Store search autocomplete shows real query prefixes
- Take the words the user's own customers use, from their site or reviews - founders often optimise for industry jargon their users never type

Pick one primary term and two or three secondary ones. Don't chase a head term a new app has no chance of ranking for.

## Presenting drafts

Give two options per field, not one and not eight. One reads as take-it-or-leave-it; eight is homework.

```
Subtitle (30 characters, shows under your app name):

  A  Invoicing for freelancers          (26)
  B  Get paid faster, freelance         (26)

A is clearer for search, B is stronger for conversion. I'd take A -
you can test B later, it's editable any time.
```

Give the character count. Say which you'd pick and why. Then fill in whatever they choose.

Everything here except the app name is editable later - say so, because founders freeze on this screen thinking it's permanent. Only the app name is reserved, and even that can be changed before first release.
